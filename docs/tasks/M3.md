## Metadata

- Tree ID: `M3`
- Status: `active` — since `2026-10-03`, `M2`'s open leaves all waiting on the director
- Roadmap lane: `ROADMAP.md` §12 M3; §5.4; §4.4; §14.4; §18 work package 10; fixtures F08–F12, F30
- Created: `2026-09-13`
- Owner: repo-local workflow

## Goal

Turn a typed description into a complete, deterministic, independently checked build plan:
direct feature matching plus realization through engine rules, joint constraint solving over
capacity, ownership, topology, privilege, placement and initialization order, and a separate
checker that re-evaluates the original requirements against the whole returned assignment.

## Non-Goals

- No optimization or optimality claim (§5.4: feasibility before optimization).
- No SAT/SMT dependency without a measured need and a recorded trust-boundary role.
- No code emission. That is `M4`.

## Acceptance Criteria (roadmap exit gate, §12 M3)

- The positive corpus produces checked plans.
- Resource oversubscription, ownership conflicts, stale assumptions, illegal placements, and
  incompatible provider combinations are rejected with explanations.
- The same locked input resolves deterministically.
- F30 shows that unreviewed generator/checker sharing, or a relevant feature/source change,
  is mechanically surfaced and blocks acceptance.
- No optimality claim is made unless separately established.

  three remarks, `target/m33-design-review/report.md`, answered below):
  - An **assignment** is a set of bindings, each a **requester**, the fact it requires and the provider that meets it.
    A requester is a task, for what the task itself writes; a service; any other declaration, for what it writes at its
    own level outside its tasks; or an adapter `M3.2` selects. So two tasks of one system are two requesters, though the
    relation reads them as one side (review D1). The relation (`docs/semantics/model.md` §7) judges each binding alone;
    these rules judge the bindings together. `M3.3` checks a given complete assignment; `M3.4` searches for one and wires
    both into `archogen check`. A platform's `uses` of a block is composition, never a binding.
  - **Consumed facts.** `deffact` gains an optional clause `use`, `shared` (the default) or `consumed`, the latter only on
    a boolean fact or a group's head, whose bare offer is `true` (D2). A consumed fact is a function a requester
    programs — an alarm — taking one of the provider's channels; a shared one is read without being taken. `/1` marks
    `relative-delay` and `absolute-deadline` consumed and every other entry shared, each with its reason in the record's
    row. A further mark is a §15 migration, since it can refuse a system accepted before.
  - **Channels.** A new entry, `channels` (domain `count`, role `guarantee`, direction `at-least`): how many requesters a
    provider serves at once through its consumed facts, one pool for all of them. A binding takes a channel when its
    requirement is met by the value `true` of a consumed fact (D2). The requesters taking channels at one provider number
    at most its `channels`; more is a conflict (F08). One needs no count; two or more where `channels` is undescribed or
    unknown is `missing-fact`, naming it. A provider that offers a consumed fact `true` and declares `channels` absent or
    `0` is `invalid-description`, judged at the provider whether or not anything binds it, as model §2 rule 1 judges a
    contradiction (D2).
  - **A service several users reach is one requester**: its realization serves them, so it is the multiplexer, and
    §5.4's semantics and bounded overhead are its obligation. The check reports that obligation with the assignment —
    the service, its users and the provider — for `M3.2`'s realization and the analysis to discharge, never dropped
    (D1). A multiplexing adapter is likewise a provider with more channels and itself one requester of one channel
    beneath it, so the count admits it with no exception; `.2`'s tests hold it with a synthetic provider until `M3.2`.
  - **Ownership.** A provider owns each `(region NAME (base B) (size S))` it offers, the bytes `[B, B+S)`: `B` a count,
    `S` a quantity of information in bytes, any other sub-clause ignored; several regions at one provider are each owned
    (D3). Two distinct providers both bound in an assignment whose owned ranges overlap are a conflict (F09: direct and
    firmware control of one comparator), except a provider and one it composes through `uses`, transitively (remark 4).
    A region without `size` at a bound provider is `missing-fact` when another bound provider owns bytes at or above its
    base; one without `base`, or with a base or size of the wrong kind, is `invalid-description`; a range past the exact
    arithmetic is `unsupported-profile`. A provider nothing is bound to owns nothing: an unused device costs nothing
    (`ROADMAP.md` §5.3). RAM, and placement into it, is `.5`'s.
  - A conflict is `infeasible-configuration`, and an unknown that blocks one assignment `missing-fact`, only once no
    assignment avoids them — `missing-fact` when some assignment is blocked by unknowns alone (`M3.4`'s to conclude;
    remark 6). Each names the provider, the requesters with their requirements' spans, and the count or the two ranges,
    with a repair direction: another provider, a sharing realization, or the fact described (§5.5).
  - Children of their own, not decided here: connectivity and interrupt routes; privilege (`SR-H11`, `SR-H12`); placement

    owning its whole device, the firmware shape of remark 5, with `M3.2`; initialization order; `ordering` (`SR-H13`). No
