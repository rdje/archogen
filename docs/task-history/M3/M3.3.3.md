  Design (`2026-10-22`), revised after one independent review (`ROADMAP.md` §14.2, trusted semantic rules: seven
  defects and four remarks, each verified against the tree, `target/m333-design-review/report.md`, answered below):
  - **An assignment is one system's** (review D3). `archogen check` judges each system of a description
    (`crates/eadl-model/src/workload.rs`) and `archogen build` takes one (`crates/archogen-s0/src/interpret.rs`); a plan
    is one system's, and `SR-H15`'s closure is "rooted at the system". So the joint rules judge one system's
    assignment: channels count and ownership compares within it, and two systems' boards, each with a timer at one
    address, never meet in an ownership check. A description of several systems is resolved system by system.
  - **Connectivity is `SR-H15`'s last clause, in the checker's form** (D5). `SR-H15` gives `M3.4` the closure and says
    "a provider on a platform the system does not use never satisfying", so the search proposes no such binding. The
    checker re-evaluates the returned assignment on its own (§5.4), so it reads the system's hardware itself and
    refuses an assignment binding a requester to a block or platform outside it: a conflict of that assignment, naming
    the requester, its requirement's span, the provider and where it is declared, and the system, with a repair —
    compose the provider into the system's platform, or bind another. The description's code is not decided here; it
    is `SR-H18`'s, over the providers that take part. Model §8 rule 6 holds as written (R9): "The description is
    `infeasible-configuration` when every assignment has a conflict, and `missing-fact` when some assignment is blocked
    by unknowns alone".
  - **A system's hardware** (D1) is the blocks and platforms reached from it along `SR-H15`'s edges: every name its
    `platform` clause holds, under `uses` or `needs`; a block or platform named by its own or its tasks' `uses` or
    `needs`; and, from each block or platform reached, every block or platform its `uses` or `needs` names, a clause of
    its own or inside `requires`, at any depth. `refines` is no edge: a refinement keeps the abstract platform's
    obligations (model §3) and does not take its blocks, so `bus.fast`, refining `bus.main`, has `bus.clock` through
    `needs` and not `bus.timer` (`docs/semantics/modules/hw.bus.eadl`). A system with no hardware binds no requester to
    a block or platform: each such binding is refused here, and what that makes of the description is `SR-H18`'s.
  - **Who a requester serves** (D2, D6, R11). The closure reaches a system's own level, its tasks, its hardware, and
    every declaration a `uses` or a `needs` of what it reaches names — services and policies included. Connectivity binds
    blocks and platforms only: an adapter `M3.2` selects and a record `M3.7` makes a provider are composed by no
    platform, so each takes the system of the requesters bound to it, and its own bindings are judged against that
    system's hardware, for `M3.2` and `M3.7` to build. That the rules say nothing of a requester the closure does not
    reach covers connectivity and routes only; channels and ownership count every requester an assignment holds (R11).
    The hardware is read from the declarations, not from `Requester.uses`, which merges a system's `platform` names with
    its own `uses` (R11).
  - **Interrupt routes, as one active core admits them** (D4, R10). A route is the path from an interrupt to the core.
    The description's half is connectivity; the controller between them, its line and context, and the delivery latency
    are the catalog's (`decision_target-platform-description.md` §2); and the analysis requires the interrupts the build
    enables to equal the declared sources plus the timer (`decision_runtime-analysis-variant.md`, *enabled sources*).
    So an assignment reports a **route obligation** for each distinct provider it relies on for an interrupt: a
    *source*, bound through `interrupt-source` at `true`, carrying the state of its own `min-arrival-separation`, since
    `SR-H14` (`M3.3.6`) may bind that fact to another provider; and a *timer*, bound through a consumed fact at `true`,
    one per provider — the analysis models one timer, so a second is the analysis's to refuse, never this rule's.
    `M4.1`'s plan discharges each, enabling those interrupts and no other, and the analysis's enabled-sources premise
    reads them; none is assumed met.
  - Not decided here, each with its reason: a controller's lines as owned resources — the description names no
    controller under this profile; a route to more than one core — `multicore` is outside it; the privilege a route
    delivers at — `M3.3.4`'s (`SR-H11`, `SR-H12`).
  - **What changes, and what wiring will move** (D7, R8). Two `M3.3.2` tests change in this leaf's commit, each with its
    reason: `f08_a_multiplexer_…`'s synthetic multiplexer is a block standing in for `M3.2`'s adapter and gains a place in

    `app.system`'s one requirement, `os.time`'s `unambiguous-horizon`, is met by no provider at all (R8).
  - Tests with literal expectations, every assignment enumerated as `M3.3.2`'s are: `hw.bus`'s edges — a platform's
